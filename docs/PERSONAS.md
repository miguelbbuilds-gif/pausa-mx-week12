## Persona A — Valeria

**Age and context:** 23. Early-career worker in Mexico City. Receives a monthly salary. Shares rent, pays her share of utilities, and already has a small monthly payment for earbuds she bought earlier. She is looking at a new phone. The store screen shows a cash price and a “meses” option. She has not been interviewed; this portrait is invented for design.

**Need, in her words:**  
“Quiero ver si me alcanza el celular sin quedarme en ceros para la renta y lo que ya debo este mes.”

**Current workaround:**  
She subtracts rent in her notes app, looks at her banking app balance, and asks a friend “¿tú crees que sí?” The installment table on the retailer site only shows the monthly quote, not her remaining money after obligations she already has.

**What might make her pay (hypothesis):**  
A way to save an evaluation she can reopen before going to the store, and later a very small paid export or shared household view — **untested**. Week 12 does not charge anyone.

**What would make her quit:**  
Being told “no compres.” Being asked to connect her bank. Tiny inputs on a cheap Android phone. Copy that sounds like a credit bureau.

**Accessibility and trust concerns:**  
She uses a mid-range Android and mobile data. She will not paste a CLABE. She needs MXN with clear thousands separators and language that does not imply the app knows her bank.

**Realistic task scenario:**  
On a Sunday evening she opens PAUSA MX, enters available money $8,400, essentials $5,200, committed payments $450, phone price $6,999, payment method contado. She reads remaining money after the purchase and the comparison with waiting. She signs in with Google and saves the evaluation to reopen on Monday in the store.

## Persona B — Daniel

**Age and context:** 27. Worker with variable income (commissions and extra shifts). Considering a computer in installments. Some months are comfortable; some are tight. He has not been interviewed; this portrait is invented for design.

**Need, in his words:**  
“Si doy enganche y me quedan mensualidades, quiero ver qué me queda hoy y qué estoy prometiendo después — sin que la app se invente intereses.”

**Current workaround:**  
Screenshots of the store’s months table, a spreadsheet with last month’s income (which is optimistic), and a habit of rounding the monthly payment down in his head.

**What might make him pay (hypothesis):**  
Reopening saved evaluations across months when income changes — **untested**.

**What would make him quit:**  
The product inventing an interest rate, scoring him, or treating last month’s income as a guarantee of next month. A chatbot that “advises.”

**Accessibility and trust concerns:**  
He needs an explicit warning that future installments cannot be judged as affordable unless future money is entered — because his income is variable. He needs to see total amount actually typed versus cash price.

**Realistic task scenario:**  
He enters available money $12,000, essentials $7,800, committed payments $1,200, computer price $14,999, installments: down payment $3,000, 12 payments of $1,250. He reads immediate remaining money, future payments created ($15,000), the difference versus cash price, the warning that future affordability is not established from this month’s cash alone, and the comparison with waiting. He saves after Google sign-in.

## What must be validated with real users

v0.1 is a hypothesis pack. Before treating either persona as evidence:

1. Whether 20–30 year olds in Mexico describe the job as “available money after obligations,” or use different words (saldo, quincena, “me alcanza”).
2. Whether they will type amounts without a bank connection.
3. Whether “Comprar ahora / Esperar” is read as information or as a hidden recommendation.
4. Whether installment users notice the cash-price vs total-entered difference.
5. Whether Google sign-in is acceptable for saving financial figures.
6. Whether the future-affordability warning is understood by people with variable income.
7. What, if anything, they would pay for — currently unknown.

No interview findings are recorded here because none exist.
